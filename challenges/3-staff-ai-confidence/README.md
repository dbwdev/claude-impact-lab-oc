# Challenge 3: Staff AI confidence

> Hands-on practice for a frontline role that shows where AI helps, where it should stop, and how
> to check and correct its output.

## The situation

Illumination Health + Home (IHH) cares for people recovering from illness while experiencing
homelessness. Frontline staff spend the shift on rounds, medication checks, vital signs and
conversations with clients. Writing it down comes last, and that record is what shows a health
plan why a client still needs care.

AI tools could take some of that load. IHH's leaders told us why it has not happened yet:

- Earlier trainings did not turn into day-to-day use.
- Staff worry about client privacy.
- Staff worry about what AI means for their jobs.
- A tool gets used only if it is easy and the person using it stays in control.

A lecture will not fix that. Ten minutes of doing it yourself, and catching the AI in a mistake,
might.

## Who you are building for

One frontline role. Pick it and stay with it. Role cards are in [`roles.md`](roles.md):

- Licensed vocational nurse (LVN) or medical assistant in recuperative care
- Referral coordinator
- Case manager

## What a useful practice experience does

1. Takes about 10 to 15 minutes on a phone or a shared workstation.
2. Uses a task the person does every week, with made-up client details.
3. Lets them see AI save real time on that task.
4. Has them catch at least one thing the AI got wrong, and say why it matters.
5. Leaves them with a short rule of thumb for when to use AI, when to check, and when to stop.

A good demo: an IHH staff member who has not seen your build picks it up, finishes the practice
without help, and finds an error in an AI draft.

## Ideas

- **Spot the seven.** Show the transcript and the draft side by side. Tap what is wrong. Get
  feedback on what you missed and why it matters to the client.
- **Fix it, then send it.** The learner corrects the draft and sees how long it took compared with
  writing from scratch.
- **Would you paste this?** Quick yes, no or ask-first cards on privacy.
- **Green, yellow, red.** A pocket guide built by the learner: tasks where AI is fine, tasks where
  you must check, tasks where AI should not be used.
- **Five prompts for tomorrow.** Role-specific prompts that need no client information.
- **Huddle kit.** A 15-minute version a supervisor can run with a team at shift change.
- **Before and after.** One question at the start and the end: how confident are you that you could
  tell when an AI note is wrong?

## Watch out for

- **Do not teach false confidence.** The point is judgment. A practice where the AI is always right
  teaches the wrong thing.
- **Do not build a scribe.** This is not a production note-taking or transcription tool, and it
  does not connect to IHH's record system.
- **Respect the worry about jobs.** Show AI taking the paperwork, not the role.
- **Keep it short and plain.** People will do this between tasks, on a phone, possibly in a second
  language.
- **Do not invent IHH policy.** Where a rule would come from IHH (which tools are approved, what
  may be entered), say "follow IHH policy" and leave a place for it.

## Ask the people in the room

What did the last training get wrong? What would make you try this on a busy day? Which mistake in
a note would get someone in trouble? Who on the team do others copy?

## Ground rules

Made-up clients only. No real client information, from anyone, for any reason. See
[`docs/ground-rules.md`](../../docs/ground-rules.md).
