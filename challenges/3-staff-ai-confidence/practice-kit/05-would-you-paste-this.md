# Would you paste this?

Ten situations for quick practice cards. The answers follow two general rules that hold in any
health setting:

- Client information goes only into tools your organization has approved for it.
- Use the least information the task needs.

**IHH's own policy decides the real answer.** These cards do not replace it. Where a card says
"ask first", build a place in your design for IHH's rule.

| # | Situation | Answer | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | You want help wording a note. You type the client's full name, date of birth and diagnosis into a free chatbot on your phone | No | Identifiable client information in a tool nobody approved |
| 2 | You ask: "How do I write a clear note about a missed medication dose?" with no client details | Yes | No client information. The AI is helping with wording |
| 3 | You paste a hospital discharge summary into an AI tool to get a short version | Ask first | It depends entirely on whether that tool is approved for client records |
| 4 | You take a photo of a wound to ask an AI what it looks like. The client's face and wristband are in the frame | No | A photo is client information too, and AI should not be assessing a wound |
| 5 | You ask an AI to turn your shift checklist into a tidy table. No names on it | Yes | Nothing about a client |
| 6 | You describe "a 58-year-old man in room 12 with a heel ulcer" to ask a question | No | No name, but enough detail that people around him would know who it is |
| 7 | You ask an AI to explain what "CIWA score" means | Yes | A general question. Check the answer against a trusted source |
| 8 | An AI drafts a note and you sign it without reading it | No | Your name is on the note. You are responsible for what it says |
| 9 | You ask an AI what dose of insulin a client should get | No | That is a clinical decision. It belongs to the prescriber |
| 10 | You paste an internal policy document into an AI tool to ask what it means | Ask first | Not client information, but it may be confidential to the organization |

## The short version for a pocket card

- **Green:** wording, formatting, explaining a term, practicing with made-up examples.
- **Yellow:** anything you will sign or act on. Read every line first.
- **Red:** client details in an unapproved tool. Clinical decisions. Signing what you have not read.
