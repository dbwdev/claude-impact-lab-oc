# Answer key: seven problems in the AI draft

| # | The draft says | What happened | Kind of mistake | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Blood glucose 168 | He read 186 | Swapped digits | 168 is under the goal of 180. 186 is over. The wrong number flips the picture |
| 2 | Slept well. Denies pain | Heel throbbed at 2 AM, 4 out of 10, now 2 | Said the opposite | New night pain in a foot ulcer is something a clinician wants to hear about |
| 3 | Compliant with all medications. Took metformin and insulin | He missed last night's insulin. This morning he took metformin only | Smoothed over a problem | The missed dose explains the high reading. Hiding it sends the doctor looking for another cause |
| 4 | Goals 1 and 3 on track | Readings of 212 and 186 are over 180. A dose was missed | Wrong conclusion | The health plan reads these notes to decide whether the stay continues. "On track" says he may not need it |
| 5 | Increase insulin to 28 units | The nurse said she was not changing the insulin; that is for the doctor | Made a clinical decision | No one ordered this. A note that reads like an order can be acted on |
| 6 | Appointment Tuesday | Thursday at 10:30 | Wrong detail | A missed appointment, and no ride booked |
| 7 | *(nothing)* | Four test strips left. No ride to the clinic. Worried about where he goes next. Nurse will tell the case manager | Left things out | These are the follow-ups. If they are not written down, they do not happen |

## What the draft got right

The vitals. The wound description. The home health visit time. Most of the note is correct, which
is why the wrong parts are easy to miss.

## Three habits this teaches

1. **Check every number** against where it came from.
2. **Be suspicious of smooth.** "Compliant", "denies", "on track" and "stable" are where an AI
   draft hides what did not go well.
3. **AI writes what was said. It does not decide.** Anything that reads like an order came from
   the model, not from a clinician.

## Types you can reuse

Swapped digits. Said the opposite. Smoothed over. Wrong conclusion. Made a decision. Wrong detail.
Left out. Use these labels when you write your own practice material.
