# Judging

## How it runs

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 4:30 PM | Submissions close |
| 4:30 to 5:00 PM | Judges visit every table. Two minutes each: show the thing working |
| 5:00 PM | Finalists present to the room. Four minutes to demo, two minutes of questions |
| About 5:45 PM | Judges score. Winners announced |

Not every team presents on stage. Every team gets seen at their table.

The panel includes the organizer and Illumination Health + Home (IHH) staff. IHH's view of what
would help on day one carries the most weight.

For Challenge 1, judges may hand you a referral packet your tool has not seen and ask you to run it.

## The scoring card

Each criterion is scored 0 to 5. Day-one impact counts double. The total is out of 25.

| Criterion | What is being scored | 5 | 4 | 3 | 2 | 1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Day-one impact** (counts double) | Is it ready to be implemented, and will it have a meaningful impact on day one? | ready and high impact | almost ready, high impact | ready but mild impact | almost ready, mild impact | not ready and/or not impactful |
| **Product** | Quality of the user experience, overall ease of use and onboarding | polished, users get it on first try | well designed, fits the workflow | pretty, usable with little learning | functional, but needs some learning | hard to use at first |
| **Idea** | Regardless of the result, how creative and impactful was the idea proposed | amazing, rare insight | creative, original, impactful | solid, but slightly predictable | common, close to what exists | weak, already exists |
| **Clarity of the demo** | Are the problem, the user and the outcome obvious? | impeccable pitch and demo | good pitch, convincing demo | ok pitch, demo worked well | weak narrative, demo hides problems | no narrative or demo failed |

## What scores well here

- **One user, one moment.** "A referral coordinator opens a new packet" beats "a platform for IHH."
- **An IHH staff member used it.** Put it in front of them before 3 PM and change it based on what
  they say. Tell the judges what you changed.
- **It shows its evidence.** Page numbers, quotes, sources, dates.
- **It knows what it does not know.** Tools that say "unknown" or "ask staff" at the right moment
  score higher than tools that always have an answer.
- **It respects the person.** Plain words. No jargon for clients. Staff stay in control.
- **It is honest.** Say what is real, what is mocked and where it fails.

## What does not score

- Slides without a working demo.
- Features that need IHH's live systems to mean anything.
- Anything built on real patient or client information. That is a disqualification.
